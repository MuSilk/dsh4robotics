# HISTORY — what the rules cost to learn

> **Not part of the in-episode reading path.** Read it when you plan a budget, or when a rule in
> `SKILL.md` looks wrong and you want to know what it was measured on. Every number in the method
> came from one of these runs.

## 1. The economics, in one paragraph

A row costs ~0.5–1 s and a depth call ~1 s, so analysis interleaved with motion burns both
budgets. Plan **~200 rows** for a grasp + carry + close, **~40 rows per 0.4 m of carry** (the carry
dominates, not the grasp; a payload the jaws barely span halves the stride), **~0 probe**. Treat a
second attempt as evidence of a mis-measurement rather than a normal cost. And check the budget
**per clause**, not per episode: a two-clause task splits it (task 21: knob 60–90, pan ~230), so
put a hard cap on each stage (knob 120, pan 300), print `remaining_episode_steps` after every
stage, and when a stage passes its cap **stop and change the geometry — never "one more push"**.
A NaN-free run that ends at 1000/1000 with the payload still on the table is strictly worse than
one that stops at 400 with a measurement saying which clause is unreachable.

**Check the budget per clause, not per episode — the 1000-row failure above is a
bookkeeping failure.** A two-clause task splits the budget: the knob is ~60–90 rows, the pan's
grasp + 0.46 m carry + region descent ~230, and the probe is 0. So put a **hard cap on each
stage** (knob 120, pan 300), print `remaining_episode_steps` from `libero_status` (0 rows, no
frames) *after every stage*, and when a stage passes its cap **stop it and change the mechanism
or the geometry** — never "one more push". A NaN-free run that ends at 1000/1000 with the
payload still on the table is strictly worse than one that stops at 400 with a measurement
that says which of the two clauses is unreachable.

Plan **~40 rows per 0.4 m of carry** — the carry dominates, not the grasp. A goto costs roughly
`max(Δaxis)/0.012` rows, but **a heavy payload halves that**: task 18's 20 cm pan ran
5–10 mm/row for the whole 0.5 m (55 rows, and the first 35 of them covered only 0.24 m). Size a
carry from `STRIDE` for a bowl and from **half of it** for anything the jaws barely span, and
read the printed residual rather than the row count. Cap calibration at one measurement and
prefer a measurement fused into a task motion. The three most expensive traps are all in §2/§3:
the 16-row jaw close (~120 rows
burned once), the pad/hand height (4 cm ≈ a whole episode), and the hand's *front* corner: 11 cm
of lead is enough to stand the arm on a 3.2 cm handle bar and it presents as a freeze (§2's third
test, §3's two-stage descent). The fourth is cheaper to avoid: **a hook whose height is wrong is
silent** — and on a *bar* 5 mm is wrong (§3). Probe it with 4–6 biased rows (§2) instead of
pulling 18 and looking.

**Task 6's numbers, in one line each** (the whole point of the section above): press at
`eef_z = bar_z0 − 0.045` = 0.9499, in y to `bar_front − 0.015`, for 22–26 rows until the y
residual is ~1 cm; probe with 6 rows of dy +0.85 / dz −0.35 and require both a panel move ≥ 5 mm
and `dz_actual ≈ 0`; then pull in 8-row bursts of the same profile, checking the drawer's own
plane after each, re-deriving `bar_front = panel + 0.0322` — 4.4 cm on the first burst, 6 cm on
the next, full stop at 0.1594–0.1609 m out. That is ~45 rows of motion for the whole open.

**When `close_grip` comes back at 0.0005, the *plan* is wrong, not the approach** — the arm
travelled, the jaws settled on nothing and the scene did not change. Stop re-aiming in the same
pose.

| # | mistake | cost | the rule that now prevents it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | the hand's height / pad offset off by ~4 cm (or read from `pads()` at a pitched pose, where it is the palm) | a whole episode (task 5: ~215 rows chasing a "freeze" at `eef_z 1.0645` = bar top − 0.082) | §Gripper: the four-box ladder, the per-pose read, and "a face at `eef_z+0.082` is what you are standing on" |
| 2 | a capped descent that closed on air (`gq 0.0015`) 23 cm above the bowl; a 45-row cap at `a[2]=−0.12` covers only 6 cm | ~120 rows + the grasp (task 17) | §Motion: size a descent from the row rate, two phases, `gq ≤ 0.0022` means you are not at the object |
| 3 | a hook whose height is wrong is silent (1.6 cm high pulled 0.198 m and moved the panel 0.000 m) | ~200 rows across two sessions (task 6) | §Grasp: `eef_z = bar_z0 − 0.045`, the press residual, and the 4–6-row biased probe |
| 4 | the corridor: a descent into running the palm into table clutter | ~200 rows (task 23), ~230 rows (task 24) | §Loop 3/4: the corridor audit, and which rim can be pinched |
| 5 | the stack ending: a hover-and-drop instead of a measured set-down | ~300 rows (task 17, four episodes) | §Verdicts: a stack is a contact clause at the rim plane, and the HELD pose is `on` |
| 6 | **a stage that comes back short, and the next stage running anyway** — a 136° rotation capped at 28 rows returned `rot_err` 0.60 (34° short) and the descent ran | a whole episode (task 26: 1000/1000, bottle on the table) | §Loop 0: every stage *returns* its verdict and a short one blocks the next; §Motion: a rotation is **3.6°/row**, so 90° ≈ 25 rows and 180° ≈ 50 — budget from the angle, then read `rot_err` |

Two more that are not mistakes but taxes: the jaw close is 16 rows (3–5 "close" rows leave the
jaws 6 cm open), and a rotation-only row walks the arm 4–8 cm, so a re-orientation is a stage, not
a row. And a *missed* close is not a no-op: it **ploughs** a free object — 5 cm in one close (plus
3.4 cm of arm) and 24 cm over two on task 26, which is more than this arm's reach margin, so a
failed pinch can end an episode by distance rather than by technique.
